📅 Heritage

JDM History

The evolution of Japanese performance cars decade by decade.

1969

The First GT-R

Nissan launches the original Skyline GT-R (PGC10), starting one of the most legendary nameplates in automotive history. It wins 52 of its first 53 races in Japan.

1989

The Gentleman's Agreement

Japanese automakers agree to cap advertised power at 280 HP — a "gentlemen's agreement" that defined a generation of cars: R32 GT-R, MK4 Supra, RX-7 FD, and NSX all launched in this era.

1992

Rally Homologation Legends

Mitsubishi launches the Lancer Evolution for WRC homologation. Subaru soon follows with the Impreza WRX STI. The Evo vs STI rivalry becomes the greatest battle in rally history.

1999

R34 GT-R — Peak JDM

The Skyline GT-R R34 V-Spec arrives — widely considered the pinnacle of the JDM golden era. Its RB26DETT engine and ATTESA AWD system remain benchmarks to this day.

2007

The Modern Era Begins

Nissan reveals the R35 GT-R, breaking the 280 HP gentlemen's agreement with 480 HP. The age of modern JDM supercars begins, alongside classics reaching collector-car status.
